Computer Science Research Focus:

My doctoral research investigates the algorithmic foundations of reliable foundation model reasoning (RLVR) and fault-tolerant distributed systems architecture:

1. Credit Assignment Geometry in Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR)

In Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), heuristic token predictive entropy weighting is commonly used for uncertainty calibration. Through rigorous 5-way matched ablation studies on GSM8K and MATH, I proved that token entropy exhibits high positive collinearity with sequence length ($r = +0.486, p < 10^{-6}$), acting as an unintended length penalty that suppresses verbose correct derivations. I formulated length-normalized trajectory credit estimators that prevent gradient distortion.

2. Fault-Tolerant Distributed Consensus in Modern C++20 (quorumshift)

At Capital One Systems Research, I designed and implemented quorumshift, an adaptive Raft consensus engine in C++20. By establishing clock-drift bounded dynamic quorum re-weighting ($\Delta$-bounded drift), quorumshift dynamically routes heartbeats and append-entries around degraded links, eliminating p99 tail latency spikes without violating state-machine linearizability.

3. Counterfactual State Continuation Protocols (recovery_eval & stateshift)

Developed difference-in-differences evaluation frameworks (recovery_eval, stateshift) that isolate genuine reasoning recovery from baseline generative capacity by conditioning continuation rollouts on frozen structural covariates.
