When Confidence Proxies Confound Reasoning Complexity

Testing estimator validity and negative controls in RLVR post-training

Sham Satish Thakare • August 2026 • 12 min read • Under review at IEEE TAI
Abstract: A diagnostic audit exposing completion-length confounding ($r = +0.486$, $N=100$) in token predictive entropy, proving MC-dropout determinism on zero-dropout LLMs, and demonstrating via preregistered negative controls that consistency weighting produced zero observed online advantage ($d = 0.00$, $N=3$ seeds) over outcome-supervised GRPO.

1. The Confounding Hypothesis

In reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) for mathematical and logical reasoning, Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) relies on outcome verification to compute advantage estimates. Recent work proposes weighting policy gradients by intermediate confidence proxies—such as token predictive entropy $H(x_t)$, sequence mean negative log-likelihood (NLL), or self-consistency clustering.

The unstated premise is that higher entropy marks critical reasoning forks requiring larger updates. However, mathematical reasoning derivations are heterogeneous in length: complex, multi-step sub-proofs naturally generate larger cumulative predictive entropy and wider logit dispersion regardless of error presence.

2. Zero-Order vs. Partial Correlation Controls

Across 100 untouched mathematical problems from GSM8K evaluated on Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct, zero-order token predictive entropy exhibits a significant positive correlation with total completion token length ($r = +0.486$, 95% CI $[+0.318, +0.627]$, $p < 0.001$).

When controlling for sequence length via partial correlation analysis, the apparent relationship collapses to non-significance ($r_{\text{partial}} = -0.092$, $p = 0.365$). This demonstrates that entropy-weighted advantage estimators primarily act as sequence-length regularizers rather than step-level difficulty detectors.

3. The MC-Dropout Determinism Failure Mode

Monte Carlo Dropout is frequently cited as an epistemic uncertainty estimator. We audited 50 forward passes per prompt across modern transformer decoder models (Qwen2.5, LLaMA-3). Because modern autoregressive foundation models omit dropout layers during standard inference configurations, token log-probabilities remain completely deterministic: $\text{Var}(\log P(x_t)) = 0.0000$ across all sampled passes. Estimators relying on nominal MC-dropout on inference-mode checkpoints produce degenerate constant variance vectors.

4. Five-Way Online Advantage Ablations

To test whether confidence weighting translates to online reinforcement learning gains, we executed a 5-way matched ablation study on GSM8K across $N=3$ random seeds. Outcome-supervised GRPO achieved $80.00\% \pm 0.00\%$ Pass@1. Confidence-Aware GRPO (CA-GRPO) achieved an identical $80.00\% \pm 0.00\%$ Pass@1, yielding Cohen's $d = 0.00$. Furthermore, inverse-length weighting and uniform random noise perturbations matched or degraded convergence stability.

5. Recommendations for Reasoning Evaluation

  1. Always report partial correlations controlling for completion length before claiming entropy corresponds to reasoning uncertainty.
  2. Verify the presence of active stochasticity before reporting Monte Carlo dropout variance.
  3. Include length-matched negative control baselines in all RLVR credit assignment publications.
