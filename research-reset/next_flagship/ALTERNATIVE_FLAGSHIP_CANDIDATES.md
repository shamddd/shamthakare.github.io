## CANDIDATE 1: INTERVENTION FRONTIERS — EMPIRICAL SUPPORT EXPANSION VS BEST-OF-N BEHAVIORAL REWEIGHTING NULLS

* **Problem**: Post-training interventions ($A_0 \to A_5$) are widely used, but it remains unknown at what task complexity threshold post-training moves from merely reweighting base-model behavior to generating true support expansion (producing valid solution trajectories with base probability $p < 10^{-6}$).
* **Why Important**: Prevents wasting massive RLVR compute on tasks where inference-time verifier search ($A_1$) or prefix steering ($A_2$) already achieves identical performance limits.
* **Closest Prior Work**: *Echo Chamber* (Zhao et al., COLM 2025; P25), *Q-Probe* (Li et al., ICML 2024; P26), *PERL* (Zhang et al., 2024; P28).
* **Precise Missing Piece**: An empirical definition of the "Behavioral Reweighting Null" ($A_1$ Best-of-$N$ with $N=10,000$) compared against minimal parameter-efficient RL ($A_3$) and full RL ($A_5$) on tasks requiring algorithmic composition steps absent in pretraining.
* **Falsifiable Hypothesis**: $H_1$: On tasks with base Pass@$10,000 = 0$, parameter-efficient RLVR ($A_3$) achieves non-zero accuracy ($> 5\%$) by generating token transition sequences whose joint base log-likelihood is significantly lower ($p < 10^{-8}$) than any trajectory generated by base policy $A_0$.
* **Smallest Decisive Experiment**: Train a 360M parameter model (SmolLM2-360M) using prefix-RLVR ($A_3$) vs full RLVR ($A_5$) on 50 synthetic multi-step modular arithmetic tasks where base Pass@$1000 = 0$. Compare against Best-of-$10,000$ verifier search ($A_1$).
* **Compute Requirement**: ~6 GPU-Hours on Apple Silicon MPS / single GPU.
* **Risk of Collision**: Moderate (P25, P28 overlap, but support-expansion null is distinct).
* **Kakade Alignment**: **High** (Aligns with foundational analysis of learning dynamics, post-training optimization, and scaling limits).
* **Independence Score**: **8/10** (Distinct formulation focused on support expansion boundaries; scored 8/10 based on clear separation from Echo Chamber's lack of support-expansion metrics).
* **Publication Potential**: **8/10** (Target venue: NeurIPS / ICLR 2027; scored 8/10 grounded in high community demand for rigorous test-time vs post-training bounds).

## CANDIDATE 2: ON-POLICY PLASTICITY COLLAPSE — PATH-LEVEL CREDIT ASSIGNMENT BOTTLENECKS IN MULTI-STEP REASONING

* **Problem**: RLVR algorithms (e.g. GRPO) use outcome rewards that distribute credit uniformly across all tokens in a rollout, causing policy gradient variance to explode as reasoning chain length $L$ increases.
* **Why Important**: Explains why RL post-training succeeds on short math problems but collapses or degrades on long multi-step reasoning chains.
* **Closest Prior Work**: *Process-Supervised Reward Models* (Lightman et al., 2023), *Credit Assignment in RL* (Sutton & Barto, 2018).
* **Precise Missing Piece**: Quantifying token-level path plasticity during on-policy RLVR without requiring expensive manual process labels.
* **Falsifiable Hypothesis**: $H_2$: Outcome-reward GRPO collapses token plasticity in intermediate reasoning steps ($L/2$) before fixing final-answer formatting tokens, causing early plateauing.
* **Smallest Decisive Experiment**: Measure intermediate activation gradient variance across reasoning step length $L \in [10, 50, 100]$ during 100 GRPO steps on Pythia-410M.
* **Compute Requirement**: ~4 GPU-Hours.
* **Risk of Collision**: High (Active topic in reasoning RLVR literature).
* **Kakade Alignment**: **High** (RL theory, credit assignment, optimization dynamics).
* **Independence Score**: **6/10** (Close to active Kakade / Harvard RL credit assignment papers).
* **Publication Potential**: **7/10** (Requires large-scale validation to stand out).

## CANDIDATE 3: AUTONOMOUS TEST-TIME COMPUTE ALLOCATION UNDER NON-STATIONARY VERIFICATION COSTS

* **Problem**: Best-of-$N$ and tree search assume fixed verifier cost per sample, whereas real-world verification (code execution, formal theorem proving) has non-stationary execution time.
* **Why Important**: Essential for building cost-efficient autonomous AI agents operating under strict execution latency and budget constraints.
* **Closest Prior Work**: *Snell et al.* (2024; P27), *Adaptive Computation Time* (Graves, 2016).
* **Precise Missing Piece**: Multi-armed bandit / optimal stopping formulation for dynamic verifier budget allocation under heavy-tailed verification latency distributions.
* **Falsifiable Hypothesis**: $H_3$: An adaptive Gittins-index stopping rule reduces total inference compute by $> 35\%$ over fixed Best-of-$N$ at equal accuracy on Python execution benchmarks.
* **Smallest Decisive Experiment**: Evaluate 500 HumanEval/MBPP coding tasks under heavy-tailed execution timeouts (10ms to 5s) comparing fixed $N$ vs adaptive stopping.
* **Compute Requirement**: ~2 GPU-Hours (CPU/GPU hybrid).
* **Risk of Collision**: Low/Moderate.
* **Kakade Alignment**: **High** (Autonomous decision making, bandit theory, optimal compute allocation).
* **Independence Score**: **8/10** (Strong theoretical framing from multi-armed bandits applied to LLM verifiers; scored 8/10 supported by zero prior papers applying Gittins-index stopping to code execution verifiers).
* **Publication Potential**: **8/10** (Target venue: ICML / COLT 2027; scored 8/10 grounded in algorithmic novelty and low compute requirement).

* **Problem**: Iterative RLVR training often leads to mode collapse where the model repeats narrow solution templates.
* **Why Important**: Prevents policy degradation during long-horizon iterative post-training pipelines.
* **Closest Prior Work**: *Rank Collapse in Deep Networks* (Dong et al., 2021), *Echo Chamber* (Zhao et al., 2025; P25).

* **Smallest Decisive Experiment**: Track top-64 singular values of residual activations during 150 GRPO steps across SmolLM2-360M.
