About Me

I am a Ph.D. student in Computer Science at Harvard University, advised by Prof. Sham Kakade and Prof. David Alvarez-Melis. I am part of the Harvard ML Foundations research group and a Kempner Institute Graduate Fellow.

My research investigates foundational machine learning, test-time training for long-context LLMs, reinforcement learning compute scaling, model composition (CALM), generalization behavior, and loss surface geometry. Previously, I worked as a Pre-doctoral Researcher at Google DeepMind with Partha Talukdar and Prateek Jain, and as a Research Scientist Intern at Meta Super-intelligence Labs (MSL) with Aston Zhang.

Featured Academic Projects & Collaborations

Augmenting New Knowledge in Language Models through Composition 07/2022 – Present

Google Research · w/ Partha Talukdar, Prateek Jain, Nitish Gupta, Sid Dalmia

  • Worked as part of a massive moonshot effort to create inclusive and equitable language representations.
  • Led a large collaboration to introduce composition of language models as a paradigm to augment new knowledge.
  • Proposed CALM: Using knowledge-specific models to augment new capabilities in a frozen language model [ICLR'24].
  • Working with Google DeepMind and the Bard team to test CALM for serving custom models to users.
Relationship between Information Distribution and Model Behavior 01/2022 – 07/2022

Technion · w/ Yonatan Belinkov, Danish Pruthi

  • Evaluating generalization of neural models is difficult: Requires creation of labeled out-of-distribution sets.
  • Employed information-theoretic metrics to study the information distribution across neurons as an intrinsic metric.
  • For the first time, showed that such intrinsic metrics strongly correlate with generalization behaviors of a model [NeurIPS'22].
Mode Connectivity in Loss Surfaces for Text Models 10/2021 – 10/2022

New York University · w/ Naomi Saphra, João Sedoc, Kyunghyun Cho

  • Analyzed linear model connectivity for multiple fine-tuned models from the same pre-trained language model.
  • Observed clusters of models that lie in separate basins within the loss surface showing identical generalization behaviors [ICLR'23].
  • Future work has utilized insights from our work for weight averaging and mechanistic interpretability.
Teacher-Student Paradigm to Evaluate Model Explanations 09/2020 – 12/2021

Carnegie Mellon University · w/ Danish Pruthi, Bhuvan Dhingra, Zachary Lipton, Graham Neubig

  • Established a student-teacher communication paradigm for automatic evaluation of explanations [TACL'22].
Grounding Language Models in Factual and Commonsense Knowledge 01/2021 – 09/2021

Adobe Research · w/ Milan Aggarwal, Sumit Bhatia, Balaji Krishnamurthy

  • Developed a framework to augment language model inputs with factual and commonsense knowledge on the fly [NAACL'22].
Neural Machine Translation for Sumerian 05/2020 – 01/2021

University of Oxford · w/ Jacob Dahl, Émilie Pagé-Perron, Niko Schenk

  • Led open-source initiative with CDLI to adapt modern NMT for extremely low-resource languages [ACL SRW'21].
