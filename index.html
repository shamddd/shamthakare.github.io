I am Sham, an independent computer science researcher and Pre-Doctoral Researcher at Capital One. My research broadly focuses on foundation model reasoning, reinforcement learning (RLVR / credit assignment), and reliable distributed systems.

At Capital One (2024–Present), I conduct research on distributed consensus algorithms, fault-tolerant replication, and C++20 concurrency under the research mentorship of Yuri Shelke (Distinguished Engineer at Capital One). Together, we developed quorumshift, a failure-domain aware dynamic quorum adaptation engine for Raft clusters that reduces p99 write tail latency by 41.3% under asymmetric network partitions.

In foundation model reasoning, my recent work investigates credit assignment in outcome-supervised reinforcement learning (GRPO), exposing completion-length confounding in token predictive entropy, and establishing state-matched difference-in-differences evaluation protocols for neural error recovery.

I graduated with a B.Tech. in Artificial Intelligence & Data Science (Cumulative GPA: 8.70 / 10.00; Final Year SGPA: 9.53 / 10.00, First Class with Distinction) from APCOER Pune (Savitribai Phule Pune University), where I was awarded the Gold Medal in Mathematics and served as President of the Social Volunteer Organization.

Research Focus

Reasoning Reliability & RLVR

Investigating credit assignment in language model reinforcement learning, exposing heuristic proxy failures, and developing length-invariant credit estimators.

State-Matched Error Recovery

Designing difference-in-differences counterfactual evaluation protocols to measure whether LLMs recover from erroneous reasoning steps.

Distributed Systems & Consensus

Developing dynamic quorum adaptation and failure-domain aware replica selection in C++20 Raft consensus clusters (collaboration with Yuri Shelke at Capital One).

Representation Plasticity (CARLS)

Analyzing gradient spectral properties and feature rank across pre-training checkpoints to predict downstream RL reward plasticity.
