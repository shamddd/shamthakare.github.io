\begin{abstract}
Learning-augmented components are increasingly deployed in distributed systems to optimize resource management and consensus quorum selection. However, under nonstationary distribution shift, un-gated learned controllers can make degraded policy decisions, inducing severe tail-latency spikes. While out-of-distribution (OOD) input-distance gates are commonly used as safety guardrails, they rely on the implicit assumption that input feature novelty implies controller failure. In this paper, we demonstrate that input novelty and controller unreliability are imperfectly coupled: input-distance gates suffer false fallbacks under benign covariate shift ($50.0\%$ false fallback rate in Q3), forfeiting performance speedups, while failing to detect harmful controller decisions under feature aliasing ($100.0\%$ missed failure rate in Q4, incurring $+80.99 \pm 1.01\,\text{ms}$ $p99$ tail-latency regret). To address this limitation, we present an online prediction-uncertainty trust gating architecture ($T_3$) for Raft consensus engines. By monitoring the predictive residual variance of expected candidate-quorum latencies, $T_3$ isolates controller reliability from input novelty. Across four nonstationary quadrant regimes (Q1--Q4, $N=20$ independent random seeds), $T_3$ eliminates false fallbacks in Q3 (absolute reduction: $-50.0$ percentage points, exact sign test $p = 9.54 \times 10^{-7}$), preserving $+2.00\,\text{ms}$ adaptive latency speedups, and eliminates missed failures in Q4 ($0.0\%$ vs. $100.0\%$), reducing $p99$ tail-latency regret to $+0.00\,\text{ms}$ ($df=19, t = 167.06, d = 37.35, p = 1.47 \times 10^{-31}$). In a physical 5-node Raft cluster testbed, $T_3$ reduces false fallbacks by $-100.0$ percentage points in benign OOD shifts, achieving a $+9.7\%$ throughput gain over naive OOD gating. Throughout all evaluations, Raft joint-consensus invariants structurally preserve $100\%$ linearizability and zero stale reads.
\end{abstract}
