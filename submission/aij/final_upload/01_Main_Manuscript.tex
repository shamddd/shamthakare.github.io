\begin{abstract}
Standard evaluations of reinforcement-learning post-training in large language models emphasize aggregate benchmark accuracy, potentially obscuring changes conditional on local reasoning state. We introduce \textbf{StateShift}, a framework for measuring target-transition recovery under matched reasoning states. In a controlled study ($N=454, 29,056$ rollouts), we observe an 11.76-percentage-point state-by-checkpoint interaction between the base and step-256 checkpoints ($\Gamma_{256}=0.1176, 95\%$ problem-blocked bootstrap CI $[0.0955, 0.1400]$). Across nine empirically evaluated checkpoints, the interaction was consistent with a non-decreasing trajectory under prespecified order-restricted analysis despite local variation in unconstrained estimates, and was already detectable at the earliest available post-training checkpoint, $t=32$ ($\Gamma_{32}=0.0333$, multiplicity-adjusted 95\% CI $[0.0011, 0.0655]$). In a separate analysis of 3,200 unperturbed rollouts, 18.19\% contained a verifier-confirmed natural reasoning error; among 582 qualifying error episodes, 180 subsequently satisfied the prespecified autonomous recovery criterion (30.93\%, 95\% problem-blocked bootstrap CI $[27.19\%, 34.82\%]$). These results show that state-conditioned evaluation exposes behavioral structure not captured by aggregate correctness alone.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Reinforcement learning from verifier feedback (RLVR) has emerged as a dominant paradigm for incentivizing mathematical and logical reasoning capabilities in large language models \cite{Shao2024Deepseekmath, DeepSeek2025Deepseek}. However, standard evaluation protocols rely almost exclusively on aggregate benchmark accuracy (e.g., MATH, GSM8K), measuring only final correctness on complete rollouts. While aggregate metrics track global model improvement, they provide limited visibility into how post-training alters local reasoning dynamics conditional on intermediate state.

Does reinforcement learning uniformly raise reasoning capabilities across all decoding paths, or does it specifically enhance the model's capacity to navigate away from locally invalid reasoning steps? Disentangling these mechanisms requires evaluating the model under controlled intermediate state perturbations.

To address this challenge, we introduce \textbf{StateShift}, a controlled experimental framework designed to measure target-transition recovery conditional on intermediate reasoning state. As illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:framework}, StateShift evaluates target-transition success under two counterfactual states: a Recovery condition initialized at a locally invalid intermediate state ($R$), and a Control condition initialized at a matched baseline state ($C$). By evaluating these conditions across reinforcement learning post-training, StateShift isolates state-selective capability gains from general accuracy improvements.

\section{Background and Related Work}
\subsection{Reinforcement Learning for Reasoning}
RLVR methods, such as Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), optimize policy models using rule-based answer verifiers \cite{Shao2024Deepseekmath, Luo2025Deepscaler}. Recent studies demonstrate substantial performance jumps in long-context reasoning models \cite{DeepSeek2025Deepseek}.

\subsection{Categorical Self-Correction Literature}
The literature on self-correction presents mixed evidence. Works evaluating verbal self-correction show that LLMs struggle to self-correct without external feedback \cite{Huang2023Can, Stechly2024Large}. Conversely, RL-based self-correction methods attempt to train multi-turn error correction \cite{Kumar2024Training}. StateShift differs fundamentally by evaluating unprompted target-transition recovery in single-rollout decoding without multi-turn prompting or external hints.

\subsection{Process Supervision and Verifiers}
Step-level outcome verifiers and Process Reward Models (PRMs) provide granular supervision for reasoning steps \cite{Lightman2023Let}. StateShift leverages deterministic python-executability verifiers to ground error classification without neural reward model noise.

\section{The StateShift Framework}
\subsection{Formalization and Estimand}
Let $s \in \mathcal{S}$ denote an intermediate reasoning state within a mathematical solution trajectory. We define two counterfactual initialization states for a given problem $p$:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Recovery Condition ($R$)}: An intermediate state containing a verifier-confirmed invalid mathematical step.
    \item \textbf{Control Condition ($C$)}: A matched control state matched for length and problem context.
\end{enumerate}

Let $\mu_{R,t}$ and $\mu_{C,t}$ denote the target-transition success rates under conditions $R$ and $C$ at RL training checkpoint $t$. The primary estimand is the difference-in-differences state-by-checkpoint interaction:
\begin{equation}
\Gamma_t = (\mu_{R,t} - \mu_{R,0}) - (\mu_{C,t} - \mu_{C,0})
\end{equation}
A positive interaction ($\Gamma_t > 0$) indicates that post-training gains are state-selective, improving transition success under invalid intermediate states more than under matched baseline states.

\section{Experimental Methodology}
\subsection{Model Lineage and Checkpoints}
We evaluate the open-source \texttt{DeepScaler} RL post-training lineage (\texttt{UWNSL/Qwen2.5-7B-deepscaler\_4k\_step\_X}), anchored by the base model \texttt{Qwen2.5-7B} at $t=0$ \cite{Yang2024Qwen25, Luo2025Deepscaler}. We evaluate nine empirically available checkpoints: $t \in \{0, 32, 64, 96, 128, 160, 192, 224, 256\}$.

\subsection{Problem Registries and Contamination Control}
Our primary controlled study uses a problem registry of $N=454$ mathematical reasoning problems. To ensure strict decontamination, we also evaluate a strict subset of $N_{\text{Strict}}=388$ problems screened for overlap.

\section{Controlled State-Conditioned Evaluation (Study A)}
In our primary confirmatory experiment ($N=454, K=16$ rollouts per cell, $29,056$ primary rollouts), we compare the base model ($t=0$) and the fully trained checkpoint ($t=256$). As detailed in Table~\ref{tab:endpoint}, the base model exhibits baseline success rates of $\mu_{R,0} = 0.3834$ and $\mu_{C,0} = 0.3892$. At checkpoint $t=256$, success rates rise to $\mu_{R,256} = 0.7039$ ($\Delta_{R,256} = +0.3205$) and $\mu_{C,256} = 0.5921$ ($\Delta_{C,256} = +0.2029$).

Between the base and step-256 checkpoints, we observe an 11.76-percentage-point state-by-checkpoint interaction ($\Gamma_{256} = \mathbf{+0.1176}, p < 0.0001, 95\%$ problem-blocked bootstrap CI $[+0.0955, +0.1400]$). \section{Natural Post-Error Recovery (Study B)}
To complement controlled perturbations, Study B evaluates unprompted natural decoding in $3,200$ rollouts ($N=200, K=16$). Figure~\ref{fig:funnel} illustrates the natural recovery evaluation funnel.

As summarized in Table~\ref{tab:natural}, $582$ of $3,200$ unperturbed rollouts contained a verifier-confirmed natural reasoning error, establishing a Natural Error Incidence ($\text{NEI}$) of $18.19\%$ ($95\%$ CI $[16.84\%, 19.50\%]$). Among the $582$ qualifying error episodes, $180$ subsequently satisfied the prespecified autonomous recovery criterion, yielding a Conditional Natural Post-Error Recovery Rate ($\text{NRR}$) of $\mathbf{30.93\%}$ ($95\%$ problem-blocked bootstrap CI $[27.19\%, 34.82\%]$).

\section{Discussion}
Our findings demonstrate that aggregate benchmark accuracy obscures crucial behavioral structure during post-training. RLVR post-training induces a state-selective transition recovery capability that operates both under controlled intermediate perturbations and during unprompted natural decoding.

\section{Limitations}
We explicitly note the following limitations:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Strict Monotonicity}: Strict pairwise monotonicity across all adjacent intervals was not established due to sampling variation ($K=2/3$ at intermediate checkpoints).
    \item \textbf{Sub-32 Step Emergence}: Exact emergence below step 32 is scientifically unidentifiable because intermediate checkpoints $t \in \{1..31\}$ do not exist.
    \item \textbf{Conditional Metric}: $\text{NRR}$ is conditional on endogenous error occurrence and must not be interpreted as an unqualified causal self-correction probability.
\end{enumerate}
